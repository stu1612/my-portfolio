The problem

Probell is a Sweden-based supplement brand with a distinctive kettlebell-shaped protein container, registered as a US company. It needed a site that looked credible to US buyers and distributors, loaded fast on phones, and captured leads reliably across regions.

Constraints

  • A small brand with one decision-maker, so every choice had to be easy to explain and cheap to run.
  • Lead capture had to work for visitors in both Europe and the US.
  • The physical packaging already existed, so the site's brand had to match it.

What I built

A static site on Astro, deployed on Vercel, with HubSpot forms for lead capture. TODO: pages, content structure and design approach.

Decision

Kept the site static with Astro: it is content-first, ships almost no JavaScript, and costs nothing to host.

What went wrong

After launch, mobile performance dropped. The cause was images stored in public/, which Astro serves as-is, skipping its build-time optimisation. Moving them into src/ and rendering them through <Image> fixed it:

src/components/Hero.astroAstro 

---
import { Image } from "astro:assets";
import hero from "../assets/whey-100.jpg";
---

<!-- Placeholder example: before, a plain <img src="/whey-100.jpg"> -->
<Image src={hero} alt="Whey 100 Protein tub" widths={[480, 960]} />

What went wrong

Mobile scores regressed after launch. Images in public/ bypassed Astro's image pipeline; moving them into src/ fixed it.

Decisions along the way

  • Forms tested across regions. HubSpot form submissions were verified from the US as well as Sweden, since the target market and the client sat on different continents.
  • Stale social links removed. Icons pointing at inactive accounts were taken off, framed to the client as a credibility and SEO issue.

Decision

Deferred a logo redesign: the existing mark is printed on the physical packaging, and changing it online alone would split the brand.
